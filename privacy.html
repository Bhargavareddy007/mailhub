MailHub

Privacy policy

Effective 23 November 2026

MailHub is an email app for Mac and iPhone that lets you read, organise and send mail from your Google accounts (Gmail and Google Workspace) in one place. This policy explains what data MailHub uses, where it is kept and how you can remove it.

The short version: MailHub talks to Google directly from your Mac and your iPhone. Your mail is kept only on your own devices and at Google. Your sign-ins and a few account details (and, so your iPhone can list them, the recipients, subject and first words of mail you schedule or snooze on the Mac) sync between your own Apple devices through iCloud Keychain, which is end-to-end encrypted. The only MailHub server is a small push server for new-mail alerts on iPhone: it holds your iPhone's notification token and the addresses of the accounts you turned alerts on for, never your mail (section 5). MailHub collects no analytics and shows no ads.

1. Data MailHub accesses

When you add a Google account and allow access, MailHub uses Google's APIs to access:

  • Your email address, name and Google account identity, to know which account you added.
  • Your Gmail messages and conversations (headers, bodies and attachments), labels, drafts, send-as addresses and their signatures, so it can show, sync, organise and send your mail. When you press Send or Schedule send, MailHub also compares the email with mail already on your device (the addresses on the sent mail of all your accounts, also when "Share suggestions across accounts" is off; the addresses on the conversation; your accounts' signatures; and which account's mail an attached file came from, which MailHub also keeps with a saved draft, as references only) to warn you if it seems to go from the wrong account or to the wrong company. This happens on your device; nothing is sent anywhere. Waiting for reply is also worked out on your device from mail MailHub already has; nothing is sent to Google or anyone else for it and nothing changes in your Gmail.
  • Your Google Contacts, your "Other contacts" and, for Google Workspace accounts, your organisation's directory, only to suggest recipients while you type an address. Suggestions come from the account you are writing from; people from one account's mail are not suggested when you write from another account unless you turn on "Share suggestions across accounts" in Settings.
  • Your Gmail vacation responder and your Gmail filters (including blocked addresses), only to show and change them for you.
  • The signature of each of your send-as addresses, to save a signature you edit in MailHub to Gmail when you choose "Also update this signature in Gmail", and to read it again with "Reload from Gmail".
  • Google Drive: files that are linked in your emails, to show and open them as the account that received the email; on the Mac, the files you browse or search in "Insert files using Drive" when you attach a file; and, only when you click "Check my other accounts", whether your other MailHub accounts can open a linked file.
  • Google Calendar: for a meeting invitation in your mail, the matching event in your primary calendar, to show and record your answer, to add the event when you answer an invitation that is not in your calendar yet, and to remove a cancelled event when you choose to.
  • Your send-as addresses ("Send mail as") and your forwarding settings (forwarding addresses and whether automatic forwarding is on), to show them in Settings, to warn you while forwarding is on, and to change them when you do so in Settings.
  • The conversations in your Trash and Spam, to delete them forever when you choose "Delete forever", "Empty Trash now" or "Delete all spam messages now" and confirm.
  • Google Tasks: the task list "My Tasks" of an account, to add an email to it when you choose "Add to Tasks" (the subject, a short snippet and a link back to the email in Gmail, which names the account's email address so Gmail opens the right account), and to show, tick, edit and delete those tasks in MailHub's Tasks panel.
  • Google Contacts, to add a sender or recipient to the contacts of the account that received the message when you choose "Add to contacts", and to change a contact when you choose "Edit contact".
  • The sharing settings of Google Drive files you put in a message with "Insert files using Drive", to check whether your recipients can open them before you send, and to share them with those recipients when you choose to.

You can decline on Google's page. Everything else keeps working, and MailHub asks again the next time you use that feature. You can remove MailHub's access at any time in your Google Account, under Security, Third-party apps and services.

3. Where your data is kept

  • Sign-in. You sign in on Google's own sign-in page; MailHub never sees or stores your password. Google gives MailHub a long-lived sign-in token for each account, which MailHub keeps in the Keychain, in MailHub's own Keychain group. Apple lets any app signed by the same developer team use such a group, so another app from MailHub's developer could be built to read it. With "Sync sign-ins" on (the default), these Keychain items sync through iCloud Keychain to your other Apple devices signed in to the same Apple Account, so the iPhone app can use the accounts you added on your Mac and the other way round. iCloud Keychain is end-to-end encrypted: Apple cannot read it. Turn "Sync sign-ins" off in Settings to keep the sign-ins on that device only (the other device then asks you to sign in again). Short-lived access tokens are kept in memory only.
  • Also in iCloud Keychain (end-to-end encrypted, your own devices only), so your Mac and iPhone show the same accounts (with "Sync sign-ins" off, these stay on each device instead): each account's name and colour in MailHub, the signatures you edit in MailHub, your account groups, the list of accounts your iPhone receives alerts for, and, so the iPhone can show the Snoozed and Scheduled folders, the conversations you snoozed and the messages you scheduled on the Mac (recipients, subject and a short snippet), and your Done marks and reminders for Waiting for reply (the account's email address and references to the conversation and message, no subject or text; in the Keychain item "MailHub scheduled and snoozed mail").
  • Only on each device, in the Keychain (never in iCloud): a random key that seals messages waiting to be sent and your "Send anyway" answers (on the Mac also your security settings), so another program cannot change them; the sign-in tokens of accounts you removed, until Google confirms they are revoked; your notification and sign-in sync settings; and, on the iPhone, the push server registration (a device secret and a random reference for each account with alerts).
  • Mail cache on the Mac. To open instantly and work offline, MailHub keeps a copy of recent mail, labels, signatures, meeting invitations found in your mail and your answers to them, your vacation responder and blocked addresses, which Google permissions each account has granted, the sender verification and mailing-list headers of your messages (used for the sender warnings, Unsubscribe and reply suggestions), whether automatic forwarding is on and to which address, the senders you unsubscribed from and the suggestions you dismissed, your "Send anyway" answers to the check before sending (the From address, the kind of question and the recipient), for each saved draft which account's mail its attached files came from and, for a reply saved in another account or a forward (or a reply to a message without a Message-ID), its conversation (references only), the conversations you marked Done in Waiting for reply and the reminders you chose (the account's email address and references to the conversation, no subject or text), whether each of your sent messages is a short closing such as "Thanks" (yes or no, for Waiting for reply), the conversations to read again from Google after a change made on another device (references only), the recipient addresses seen in each account's mail (kept per account), the Google Contacts entries you added or edited in MailHub (only their Google ID, so MailHub finds them again), the sender domains you chose "Don't ask again for links from" (kept on each device separately, never sent anywhere), and your MailHub settings, in a database inside its own sandboxed storage on your Mac (~/Library/Containers/com.bhargava.mailhub). Attachments you open are cached there too, images shown in your mail stay in the app's web cache there until MailHub next starts or the account is removed, and which groups and labels you opened or closed in the left menu is kept there as well.
  • Mail cache on the iPhone. The iPhone app keeps the same kind of cache in its own storage on your iPhone. It is protected by iOS data protection (attachments and previews can only be read while the iPhone is unlocked; the database is available after the first unlock so new mail can arrive) and it is excluded from iCloud and computer backups. Attachment previews are deleted after a day and cached attachments after 7 days.
  • Profile photos. Your Google profile photo is downloaded from Google and kept on your device to show in MailHub. Profile photos of your contacts are downloaded from Google and kept on your device to show next to their messages.
  • Files you save. When you download or save an attachment, it is written where you choose (Downloads by default on the Mac).
  • Google Drive files you open are kept in a temporary folder inside MailHub's own storage, so opening one again is instant, and deleted the next time MailHub starts. When you choose Download or Save as, a copy is written where you choose.
  • Camera, Face ID and printers. The iPhone app asks for the camera only when you choose Camera to take a photo for an email you are writing; the photo is attached to that email. It uses Face ID only when you turn on Require Face ID. On the Mac, printing can make macOS ask whether MailHub may find printers on your local network.

MailHub keeps no copy of your mail outside your own devices and Google. The only data a MailHub server holds is the notification information described in section 5.

4. Who receives your data

  • Google. MailHub connects to Google's sign-in and API services (accounts.google.com, oauth2.googleapis.com, gmail.googleapis.com, people.googleapis.com (also to save contacts you add or edit), tasks.googleapis.com for Google Tasks, and www.googleapis.com for Google Calendar and Google Drive) and loads profile photos from lh3.googleusercontent.com to lh6.googleusercontent.com and, on the Mac, Drive thumbnails from those hosts, lh0.google.com to lh9.google.com, docs.google.com or drive.google.com, to fetch your mail and apply the actions you take. When you choose "View in Google Calendar", open a Drive file in your browser as one of your accounts, or open Gmail's settings, MailHub opens the Google page (calendar.google.com, docs.google.com or drive.google.com through Google's account chooser at accounts.google.com, or mail.google.com) in your web browser, with that account's address in the link so Google opens the right account. Google's handling of your data is covered by Google's own privacy policy.
  • Apple, through iCloud Keychain (end-to-end encrypted, see section 3) and, if you use alerts on iPhone, Apple Push Notification service (section 5).
  • The people you email receive the messages you send, as with any email app. If you choose "Share and send" for Drive files you inserted, Google gives those recipients access to those files; Google sends them no email of its own. When you answer an invitation whose organizer does not use Google Calendar, MailHub sends your answer to the organizer as a standard calendar reply email from the address that was invited (its calendar part names MailHub as the program that wrote it). Every email MailHub sends carries a header, X-MailHub-Id, with a random number, so MailHub can find its own message in Sent if a send is interrupted; recipients can see it, and it says nothing else about you.
  • Senders you unsubscribe from. Unsubscribe is offered only when Gmail's sender checks confirm the sender, and acts only when you choose it on a message and confirm. If the message offers one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058), MailHub sends a request to the sender's own secure (https) unsubscribe address from the message's List-Unsubscribe header. The request's body is only the words "List-Unsubscribe=One-Click", sent with the headers Content-Type: application/x-www-form-urlencoded and User-Agent: MailHub: no cookies, no Google sign-in and no other data, though like any web request it shows the sender your internet address. If the list asks for an unsubscribe email instead, MailHub shows you the exact address, subject and text first and sends it through Gmail from your address that received the newsletter only after you confirm. Otherwise it opens the sender's unsubscribe page in your web browser after telling you.
  • Translation stays on your device. MailHub detects a message's language and translates it with Apple's on-device frameworks (NaturalLanguage and Translation). The text of your mail is not sent to Google, Apple or anyone else for this. If a language is not on your device yet, the system asks you to download it from Apple; that download contains no mail.
  • Senders of emails with images. By default MailHub loads images hosted on the web only in emails whose sender Google's checks confirm, and holds them back in every other email and in your own sent messages, drafts and scheduled messages, also in replies, forwards and printouts; the email's markup cannot change this. In Settings you can choose Ask (images wait in every email until you show them, or always show them for senders you choose while Google's checks confirm them) or Load for everyone. Google's checks confirm who sent an email, not that the sender is harmless. When images are displayed, they are loaded from the sender's servers over secure (https) connections only, without saying which page they appear on, and images that look like tracking pixels (tiny or hidden images, or images from known tracking services) are skipped, on the Mac, on the iPhone and in printouts. Like any web request, loading an image shows the sender your internet address and that the message was opened. While images are held back, the email's own fonts are not loaded either. Printouts never load the email's own fonts. When you write from an address whose signature contains images hosted on the web, the Mac's compose window loads those images from where your signature points. Links you click open in your web browser; MailHub shows where a link really goes and asks first when its text names a different website anywhere in it (for example "Secure login at bank.example" leading somewhere else, also when the name is written with look-alike letters or punctuation, follows other words or punctuation, or is split across table cells), when its text names a website but the email draws it in a way MailHub cannot check as plain text (for example with shadows, overlays, its own fonts or unusual colours), when the email's styling hides, moves or changes part of the link's text or the text holds characters you cannot see (such as zero-width or very thin spaces), when the email draws other text or a picture over or under the link or MailHub cannot tell that the link's text stands out from what is behind it (for example on a gradient or a background picture), when the link shows a picture (unless the picture is not displayed and its description, shown instead, names the link's own website), when its text is too long to check, when the sender could not be verified (including when Google's checks are missing), and before a link opens another app (phone, FaceTime or Maps on the Mac; phone, Messages or Maps on the iPhone). The same check comes first for Google Drive links that MailHub opens itself, for the Google Drive file cards under a message and their menus, for the Mac's Drive menu on a link and for Cmd-click on a link in a message you are writing. On iPhone, MailHub shows no link preview for links in email, so pressing a link never loads its page. With "Ask before opening links from outside senders" turned on in Settings (off unless you or your organisation turn it on), every link in an email from outside your own companies asks first, naming the website it goes to and showing the whole address, and an email address link asks before a new message opens, showing who it would go to and any subject and text it fills in; your companies are the domains your organisation lists, or else the domains of your accounts (not public mail services such as gmail.com), and an email counts as theirs only when Google's checks verify the sender. You can choose "Don't ask again for links from" a verified sender's domain; that list stays on your device and is never sent anywhere.
  • Software updates (Mac). The Mac app checks for new versions at bhargavareddy007.github.io and downloads signed updates from github.com (which hands the file over from GitHub's download host, a githubusercontent.com address). These requests contain no account or mail data; like any web request they show your internet address and the app version.
  • The MailHub push server, only if you use new-mail alerts in the iPhone app, as described in section 5.
  • Apple TestFlight (iPhone test versions). While the iPhone app is tested through Apple's TestFlight, Apple shows the developer, in App Store Connect, the tester's name and email address (as invited), device, iOS version and number of sessions and crashes, and gives the developer crash reports of the app and the feedback a tester chooses to send (which can include a screenshot). This is Apple's TestFlight service, not code in MailHub. A screenshot shows what was on the screen, mail included, so send only what you are happy for the developer to see.
  • Nobody else. MailHub does not sell, rent, share or transfer your data, and does not use any analytics, advertising, tracking or crash-reporting service.

5. New-mail alerts on iPhone

iOS does not let an app keep checking mail in the background. To tell you about new mail right away, the MailHub iPhone app uses a small push server run by the developer (hosted on Railway, in its EU West region in Amsterdam, the Netherlands), together with Google Cloud Pub/Sub and Apple Push Notification service (APNs). This applies only to the accounts you turn alerts on for in the iPhone app.

  • How it works. When you turn alerts on, your iPhone proves to the push server that it is signed in to each of those accounts, with a short-lived identity token from Google that the server checks against Google's public keys and does not keep. MailHub then asks Gmail to send a notice to the developer's Pub/Sub topic when that inbox changes; your Mac helps keep these requests renewed for the same accounts, and never starts them for any other account. Gmail's notice contains the email address and a change number (Gmail's history ID). Pub/Sub passes it to the push server, which asks Apple to deliver a "New mail" alert to the iPhones registered for that address. The alert carries only a random reference, not your address or any mail. Your iPhone then fetches the new message directly from Google with its own sign-in and shows the alert, with the sender and subject unless you turned that off in Settings (then it reads only which messages are new, not their sender or subject). To decide whether to show a sender's name alone, it may also search your Sent mail at Google for that address; nothing new leaves your device. Each new email is an alert of its own; a later change never replaces an alert you have not read. When a change brings no new mail (for example a message read or archived on your Mac), no alert sounds or shows a banner, but a silent "Mailbox updated" notice stays in Notification Center until the next alert or until you open MailHub. Your iPhone keeps no text of your messages for alerts, only Gmail's change number and the identifier of the last message it announced for each account.
  • What the push server stores. Your iPhone's notification token (an identifier Apple issues so notifications can reach the app), the email addresses of the accounts you turned alerts on for with a random reference for each and the Google account number of each of those accounts (an identifier Google gives every account; it ties the registration to that account, so if an address is ever given to someone else, your devices stop receiving its alerts), a scrambled (hashed) form of a secret your iPhone uses to change its registration, whether the app is a development or a release build, and the times the device registered, last updated its registration and last received an alert.
  • What it never receives. The content of your email (subjects, senders, message text and attachments), your contacts, your Google password, and the sign-in and access tokens that open your mailbox (it only sees the short-lived identity tokens described above, which prove who owns an address and cannot read mail, and does not keep them). The push server cannot read your mail.
  • Why. Only to know which iPhone to alert when Gmail reports a change for one of your accounts.
  • Who else sees it. Google, whose Pub/Sub service carries Gmail's notice; Apple, which delivers the alert (it contains no address or mail); and Railway, the hosting provider, whose network logs may record the internet address your iPhone connects from. The server's own logs record the app version and scrambled (hashed) forms of addresses and tokens, made with a secret key kept only on the server; they never record the addresses, tokens or Google account numbers themselves. The server keeps internet addresses only in memory, to limit request rates, until it restarts or needs the room, and never stores them.
  • How long it is kept. Until your iPhone removes its registration (when you remove an account or turn alerts off, which also asks Gmail to stop sending notices for that account), or until Apple reports that the notification token is no longer valid, for example after you delete the app. An address is also removed from a device when more than five devices register it (the device that registered it least recently loses it) or when another Google account proves it owns the address. In any case a device is deleted once your iPhone has not registered again for 90 days, or 30 days if it has never received an alert (the app registers again each time it starts). The record, with the Google account number of each address, is then deleted, and overwritten in the server's database file.

6. Limited Use of Google user data

MailHub's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular:

  • Google user data is used only to provide and improve MailHub's user-facing email features described above.
  • It is not transferred to anyone, except to Google to carry out your requests, to your own iCloud Keychain (end-to-end encrypted) to share your sign-ins and account details between your own devices, to a sender's own unsubscribe address when you choose Unsubscribe on that sender's message, to the MailHub push server and Apple to deliver the iPhone alerts you turn on, or as required by law.
  • It is not used for advertising, including personalised or retargeted ads.
  • No person reads your data. Your mail never leaves your devices except to go to Google, so the developer has no access to it. The only Google user data on the developer's systems is the email addresses of the accounts you turn iPhone alerts on for with Google's account number for each, and Gmail's change notices for those accounts passing through the developer's Pub/Sub topic (section 5).
  • It is not used to build or train AI or machine learning models.

7. Keeping and removing your data

  • Remove an account in MailHub (Settings, Accounts, on the Mac or the iPhone): MailHub asks Gmail to stop its change notices, revokes MailHub's access with Google (if Google cannot be reached, it tries again the next time MailHub starts), deletes the account's sign-in from the Keychain and, while "Sync sign-ins" is on, from iCloud Keychain, removes the account from your other devices, and deletes the account's cached mail, attachments, invitations, recipient suggestions, profile photos and settings from the device (on the iPhone, the notification extension's last-change mark and last announced message for that address are deleted at the next push while at least one account still has alerts on, or when the app is deleted). Nothing changes in the Google account itself. Two things stay: the account's synced Keychain item is replaced by a short removal note (the address, its name and colour in MailHub and its photo address) so your other devices remove the account too, and device-wide lists that are not tied to one account (senders whose pictures you always show, templates, trusted link domains, recent searches) keep what you added until you clear them.
  • Delete the Mac app. To remove everything, first remove each account in MailHub, then delete MailHub from Applications. If you delete the app without removing accounts, also delete the folder ~/Library/Containers/com.bhargava.mailhub and the MailHub items in the Keychain Access app. Their names all start with "MailHub": "MailHub (address)" (a sign-in), "MailHub account (address)", "MailHub account groups", "MailHub scheduled and snoozed mail", "MailHub settings", "MailHub push", "MailHub notifications (mac)" or "(iphone)", "MailHub outbox key", "MailHub settings seal" and "MailHub sign-in to revoke"; some exist only on one device or only for a while. Sign-ins synced through iCloud Keychain stay on your other devices until you remove the accounts there.
  • Delete the iPhone app. First remove each account in the app. Deleting the app deletes its mail cache, but iOS keeps Keychain items after an app is deleted, and synced sign-ins stay in iCloud Keychain, so remove the accounts in MailHub (on the iPhone or the Mac) or revoke access at Google.
  • Revoke access at Google at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. MailHub then stops syncing that account and asks you to sign in again; the mail already on your devices stays there until you remove the account.

Cached data is kept on your devices for as long as the account is in MailHub.

8. Security

MailHub signs in with OAuth 2.0 and PKCE and talks to Google only over encrypted HTTPS connections. Sign-in tokens are kept in the Keychain. The Mac app runs in the macOS App Sandbox and shows email in a restricted view that cannot run scripts. The iPhone app protects its data with iOS data protection, hides its content in the app switcher and can require Face ID. MailHub warns you when Google's sender checks show that a message may not come from who it says, or could not confirm who sent it (and then shows no contact photo for the sender), including mail that pretends to come from your own address, and asks before opening a link whose text names a different website than where it goes (and, if you turn it on, before every link from outside your companies).

9. GDPR (users in the European Union)

This section applies when MailHub is used by an organisation or person in the EU or EEA. It is a draft for review and is not legal advice.

  • Who is responsible for what. Your organisation (or you, if you use MailHub for yourself) is the controller of the email and account data that MailHub handles on your devices. MailHub's mail, contacts, calendar, Drive and sign-in data travel only between your devices and Google, under your own agreement with Google; the developer of MailHub never receives them. The developer receives personal data only through the optional push server for new-mail alerts on the iPhone (section 5) and, while the iPhone app is tested, through Apple's TestFlight (section 4: the tester's name and email address, crash reports and the feedback a tester chooses to send), and processes it on your organisation's behalf as a processor, under a data processing agreement when your organisation asks for one.
  • What the push server holds. Only for accounts with iPhone alerts on: the Gmail address and its Google account id, the iPhone's Apple push token, a hash of the device's secret, whether the app is a development or a release build, and when the device registered, last updated its registration and was last alerted. It is told when an inbox changes (never who wrote or what), and it never receives the content of any email, any access or refresh token, contacts or calendars. Its logs record the app version and use keyed hashes instead of addresses and push tokens.
  • Purpose and legal basis. The push server exists only to wake the iPhone app when new mail arrives, at the user's request (turning alerts on for an account). It is not used for anything else: no analytics, profiling, advertising or sale of data.
  • How long. A registration is deleted when alerts are turned off, the account is removed in MailHub, Apple reports the device token as no longer valid, or after 30 days for a device that never received an alert, and after 90 days without a new registration otherwise (the app registers again each time it starts). An address also leaves a device when another Google account proves it owns the address, or when a sixth device registers it. Deleted records are overwritten in the server's database file. Google Pub/Sub keeps an undelivered change notice for at most 1 day. Server logs are kept for at most 90 days by the hosting provider.
  • Where. The push server runs on Railway (Railway Corporation, United States) in its EU West region (Amsterdam, Netherlands). Change notices pass through Google Cloud Pub/Sub, and alerts through Apple's push service (Apple Inc., United States). Transfers outside the EEA rely on the providers' Standard Contractual Clauses and, where they are certified, the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.
  • Sub-processors. Railway (hosting), Google Cloud (Pub/Sub, sign-in), Apple (push notifications, TestFlight while the iPhone app is tested). Your organisation is told before a sub-processor is added or changed.
  • Your rights. You can delete what the push server holds about you yourself: turning off alerts for an account, or removing the account in MailHub, deletes its registration as soon as your iPhone reaches the server. For any other request (access, correction, deletion, objection, a copy of your data) write to qa@fyes.eu; requests are answered within one month. You can also complain to your data protection authority (in Greece, the Hellenic Data Protection Authority, dpa.gr).
  • Security and breaches. The push server is reached only over HTTPS, runs without administrator rights, checks Google's signature on every change notice and a Google identity token for every address a device registers, and is administered only through the developer's own Railway, Google Cloud and Apple accounts. If a breach affects personal data, your organisation is told without undue delay and within 72 hours of the developer becoming aware of it.
  • Without the push server. An organisation that does not want any data at the developer can leave iPhone alerts off for every account (no managed setting enforces this today): MailHub then talks only to Google, Apple (iCloud Keychain, TestFlight and language downloads for translation), GitHub for the Mac's updates, and the senders' servers named in section 4. While the iPhone app is tested through TestFlight, Apple still gives the developer the TestFlight data above.
